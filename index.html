Whale Shark Jelly.

A soft-body study in two layers of cast gel — one clear, one milk. Pull it, tuck it, feed it.

Notes

The body is a signed-distance whale shark: an elliptical section swept along the spine, three low ridges down each flank, fins cut from one recipe — a triangle with a convex leading edge, a falcate trailing edge and a tip that thins away.

A hex lattice is snapped to that surface and split into five tetrahedra a cell. Each tet is solved with XPBD: co-rotational shape matching plus a volume constraint, with firm fins, edge damping and a frictional table.

The mouth is one lattice layer left out of the mesh, so the lips are only joined behind. A gulp shortens the upper fibres of the jaw until it tips; gills flare the same way. Light is refracted through the gel and absorbed with Beer–Lambert; every marking is painted in rest space, so it stays on the jelly as it wobbles.
